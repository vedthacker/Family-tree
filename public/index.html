Our roots

Who we are, and where the thread begins

The Lohanas are a trading community of Gujarat, Kutch and Sindh who, by tradition, trace themselves to the Raghuvanshi (Suryavansh) Kshatriyas — the line of Lord Rama. Community memory holds that they descend from Lav, Rama’s son, and from the fortress-city called Lohkot / Lohargadh on the north-western frontier.

Over centuries the family’s forebears became merchants, bankers and mariners — moving down the Indus into Sindh, and later across the Rann and the sea into Kutch and Saurashtra. Each family keeps its identity through its Nuk (clan), its Gotra, its Kuldevi, and the books of the hereditary genealogist, the Barot.

Migration timeline

Four chapters of a very long journey

A map of the community’s broad path. Where our own branch fits in each chapter is for us to confirm.

  1. 1

    Ancient – medieval · North-west frontier

    Lohargadh & the Punjab

    Tradition places the Raghuvanshi forebears at Lohkot (Lohargadh), linked in memory with the Lahore region. Pressure from invasions and shifting kingdoms set families moving south along the river valleys.

    TraditionAsk: does our family tell of Lav, Lahore, or “Lohar-gadh”?

  2. 2

    Medieval · Sindh & the Indus belt

    The traders of Sindh

    In Sindh, Lohana families rose as merchants, bankers and administrators in towns along the Indus and the coast. Devotion to Jhulelal (Uderolal) grew among Sindh-rooted families. Political upheavals under successive dynasties pushed families further east and south.

    Widely documentedAsk: any Sindhi words, surnames or a town like Thatta in family talk?

  3. 3

    c. 12th – 19th century · Kutch & Saurashtra

    Settling Kutch & Halar

    Waves of Lohana families crossed the Rann and the sea into Kutch (Bhuj, Mandvi, Mundra, Anjar) and Halar / Saurashtra (Jamnagar region). Mandvi’s port tied families to trade with Arabia and East Africa. Colonial-era gazetteers describe Lohana settlements throughout.

    Widely documentedAsk: which Gam does our Barot record for us?

  4. 4

    19th century – today · Modern movements

    Mumbai, Africa & beyond

    Families moved to Mumbai and Karachi, to East Africa (Zanzibar, Mombasa, Nairobi, Kampala), and, from the 1960s–70s, to the UK, Canada, the USA and the Gulf. After 1947, new settlements such as Gandhidham grew in Kutch.

    To confirm — our familyAsk: who left first, where, and when?
